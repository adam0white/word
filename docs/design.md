# His Word

You hold the notebook of your uncle Nuri, an Istanbul tailor who died last week. He promised Halil steady morning repair work. He also promised Rıza that the same floor would hold his delivery cart all day. Neither promise grants property or a job. Ferda wants to rent the empty shop from Osman and keep it open. The four living people must agree to a workable arrangement before Osman leaves to meet another tenant.

## What you win and lose

Win an explicit agreement on work hours, cart storage, and rent that still works a month later. Lose when the meeting ends without agreement. Your own travel fund can cover a rent gap, at a personal cost. Each conversation, letter trip and proposal uses the time before Osman's appointment. The clock advances with decisions; this is a deliberate first-slice pacing choice so reading speed does not decide the result.

There are two honest settlements. A morning repair service needs a cleared floor, outside cart storage, and your cash guarantee. A late service uses a supplier order described in Nuri's letter and costs less, but Halil must agree to work later. Fetching that letter costs six minutes. It is mentioned on the first screen. Missing it never reverses a win. A false private assurance can get Halil or Rıza to consider terms sooner. They compare your statements before signing. HF records YOU as the source; a contradiction reduces trust in you. You can disclose and repair the proposal at a material cost. No forced secret reversal occurs after signing.

## Technology and boundaries

Ink through inkjs authors framing and one set of choices at a time. Strict TypeScript holds the host state and the published HF 3.2.0 actors. No React, panels, canvas, server or database. Ink external functions only read cached host projections. Input handlers apply each command once, outside Ink evaluation. Ink never calls converse, perceive, contradicted or a host reducer. Replay reconstructs the initial actors from a seed and replays the input log, including Ink choice paths. Export pins content edition, HF pin, cast data, complete state and final hash. No Ink randomness is used.

HF owns actors, testimony, belief sources and trust changes. The host authors practical consent conditions and an immutable proposal protocol. Consent is an explicit host assumption, not an HF persuasion prediction: requirements must be met, assurances must be compared, and each living signatory consents to the exact proposal id. A counterproposal clears consents. Duplicate signing is a no-op. No home-grown memory drift, trust formula or actor psychology enters the host. No enableRetellingDrift in this slice.

## Review decisions

Apply both reviews: practical bargaining, conflicting real promises, a letter known from the start, costly access, actionable refusal reasons, and player-source deception. Drop property transfers, tools, debts owed by Nuri, bequests, shares and legal determinations entirely. These are agreements for future work and a new tenancy between living people. The game makes no religious or legal ruling.

The original twelve-day death watch is replaced by one bounded meeting after the death. This follows Astra's practical table and Opus's objection that the first slice would otherwise depend on unavailable drift. The month-later page verifies only the practical agreement. Halil still rents his own workshop from Osman and still owes his existing rent; the proposed job is additional paid repair work. That workshop is never threatened here. Nuri is never introduced beside Nuran; Nuran is not a story device in this game.

## Cast and setting
